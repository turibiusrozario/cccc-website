How this site is designed for access

We built this site to be easy to read and predictable, including for autistic people and people with other cognitive or sensory needs. This page lists what we did and why.

What we did

  • Large text and spacing. The base text is 20 px (18 px on small phones). Lines are short, and there is space between paragraphs. Browser zoom and text-size settings work.
  • Light and dark. The site follows your device setting. There is no switch to find or learn.
  • High contrast, calm colors. Text meets the WCAG 2.2 AAA contrast level (7 to 1). The background is warm off-white, not pure white, to reduce glare.
  • No movement. Nothing flashes, slides, plays, or changes by itself. There are no pop-ups or carousels.
  • Same layout on every page. The header, the three menu items, and the footer never move or change.
  • Literal, plain wording. We avoid idioms and jokes. We explain words such as "polycrisis" and spell out "CCCC" on first use.
  • Clear buttons and links. Links are underlined. Buttons are at least 48 px tall. Anything that opens a new tab says so in words and with an icon.
  • You choose when to see more. Items say "Click to show more" and open only when you select them.
  • Updates are polite. If new content appears while you are reading, the page does not rearrange itself. A bar at the bottom asks if you want to show it. You can say "Not now".
  • Works with keyboards and screen readers. There is a skip link, headings in order, and a visible focus outline.

What this is based on

  • Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 (opens in a new tab), the W3C standard.
  • Making Content Usable for People with Cognitive and Learning Disabilities (opens in a new tab), W3C guidance with design patterns.
  • Scope: how different disabilities affect web user experience (opens in a new tab), practical guidance on autism and the web.

Most autism-specific web guidance comes from expert practice and lived experience, not from controlled trials. We treat it as good guidance, not proof.
